## R1 — Reporting perimeter and business

- **R1.1 [FACT]** TSMC is a pure-play foundry: it manufactures customer-designed semiconductors and does not design, manufacture or market semiconductor products under its own name. It provides process technology, mask making, wafer manufacturing, packaging, testing, computer-aided design support and the Open Innovation Platform ecosystem. [S1, Item 4; S2, chapters 1–2 and 5; FY ended 2025-12-31]
- **R1.2 [FACT]** TSMC has one operating and reportable segment, the foundry segment. The segment includes manufacturing, sales, packaging, testing, computer-aided design of integrated circuits/other semiconductor devices, and mask manufacturing. Separate operating profit, assets, capex or cash flow for process nodes, platforms, wafer fabrication and advanced packaging are not reported. [S1, Note 38; as of 2025-12-31]
- **R1.3 [FACT]** TSMC manufactured 12,682 products using 305 process technologies for 534 customers in 2025. [S2, shareholder letter and §5.1.2; FY2025]
- **R1.4 [FACT]** TSMC defines “advanced technologies” in its revenue reporting as 7nm and below. “Foundry 2.0,” used for addressable-industry discussion, also includes packaging, testing, mask making and non-memory IDMs; it is not a reported financial segment. [S2, §§2.2.2 and 2.2.4; FY2025]

## R4 — Capacity and technology roadmap

- **R4.1 [FACT]** FY2025 shipments were 15.0m 12-inch-equivalent wafers, versus 12.9m in FY2024; managed annual capacity exceeded 17m 12-inch-equivalent wafers at 2025 year-end. Capacity increased by approximately 0.5m wafer equivalents during 2025. [S2, shareholder letter, §2.2.1 and §6.1.4; FY2025]
- **R4.2 [FACT]** Capacity utilization was described qualitatively as higher in FY2025 and slightly higher sequentially in Q2 2026; an exact company-wide or fab/node utilization rate was **not obtained**. [S1, Item 5; S4/S6; through 2026-06-30]
- **R4.2A [DEDUCTED]** Shipment divided by year-end capacity is not a valid utilization estimate because capacity is a point-in-time equivalent measure and node/wafer starts/cycle times differ.
- **R4.3 [FACT]** N2 entered high-volume production in Q4 2025 with what management described as good yield and was in a steep ramp during 2026; it contributed 3% of Q2 2026 wafer revenue. [S2, shareholder letter; S3/S6; through 2026-06-30]
- **R4.4 [FACT]** N2P and A16 volume production was scheduled for 2H 2026. A16 builds on N2 design rules and adds backside power delivery (“Super Power Rail”) for HPC-oriented power/performance/density benefits. [S2, shareholder letter and chapter 5; roadmap as of 2026-04-16]
- **R4.5 [FACT]** A14 risk production was scheduled for 2027 and volume production for 2028. Management’s targets versus N2 were 10%–15% speed gain at equal power, 25%–30% power reduction at equal speed, and close to 20% density gain. These are roadmap targets, not measured production economics. [S6; 2026-07-16]
- **R4.6 [FACT]** CoWoS-S had been in volume production for several years; CoWoS-R was in its third volume-production year and CoWoS-L in its second in 2025. The 5.5-reticle CoWoS-L configuration targeted qualification in 2026, while 9.5-reticle development was progressing. The annual report separately expected larger-reticle CoWoS-L products to begin volume production in 2026, but the exact configuration and production status at the cutoff were **not obtained**. [S2, chapter 5; roadmap posted 2026-04-17]
- **R4.7 [FACT]** Management said advanced-packaging capacity remained sufficiently tight to limit customer growth in Q2 2026. The absolute CoWoS/advanced-packaging capacity, utilization, backlog and customer allocation were **not obtained**. [S6; 2026-07-16]
- **R4.8 [FACT]** TSMC-SoIC 3nm stacking and InFO-PoP products with backside redistribution layers entered volume production in 2025. First-generation logic-only SoW entered volume production in 2024; logic-plus-HBM SoW remained in development. COUPE silicon-photonics production had started by the Q2 2026 call and management expected it to ramp, but revenue contribution was **not obtained**. [S2, chapter 5; S6; through 2026-07-16]
- **R4.9 [FACT]** Management said TSMC was building 13 leading-edge and advanced-packaging fabs in Taiwan over the next several years and planned three additional N3 fabs—one each in Taiwan, Arizona and Japan—while converting N5 tools to N3 support in Taiwan and using flexible capacity across N7/N5/N3. It announced an additional US$100bn Arizona investment for several logic fabs at N2 and below plus advanced-packaging fabs; timing and annual capacity were **not obtained** because deployment depends on demand. [S6; 2026-07-16]
- **R4.10 [FACT]** Higher-value mature-node expansion included JASM Fab 1 in Japan for CMOS image sensors and ESMC in Germany for automotive/industrial applications. Management described shortages in AI-related power-management IC and sensor areas but not broad commodity mature-node strength. [S6; 2026-07-16]

## R6 — Margin and cost structure

- **R6.1 [FACT]** Management attributed Q2 2026’s 150bp sequential gross-margin increase to cost improvement and slightly higher utilization, partly offset by overseas-fab dilution. Q3 midpoint gross margin was expected to fall 170bp sequentially, primarily because the steep N2 ramp was expected to dilute margin by approximately 3–4 percentage points. [S6; 2026-07-16 outlook]
- **R6.2 [FACT]** Management expected overseas-fab ramp dilution to corporate gross margin of 2–3 percentage points in early stages, widening to 3–4 points in later stages over the next several years. This is a corporate-level management outlook; site-level economics were **not obtained**. [S6; 2026-07-16]
- **R6.3 [FACT]** Semiconductor fabs are fixed-cost intensive; lower utilization leaves fixed costs over fewer wafers, while higher utilization improves absorption. Other disclosed margin drivers include node mix/ramp yields, pricing, productivity/cost improvement, overseas-fab costs, depreciation, foreign exchange, raw-material/equipment/utility/labor inflation and natural-disaster losses. [S1, Items 3 and 5; S6; through 2026-07-16]
- **R6.4 [FACT]** Q2 2026 R&D was NT$73.146bn and sales, general and administrative expense was NT$25.836bn, within NT$98.982bn total operating expenses. FY2025 R&D/G&A/marketing under IASB IFRS were NT$246.427bn/NT$82.304bn/NT$16.918bn. [S1, F-6; S4; FY2025 and quarter ended 2026-06-30]
- **R6.5 [FACT]** Cost per wafer by node, yield by node, cycle time, depreciation by node/site, packaging gross margin, wafer-fab gross margin and customer profitability were **not obtained**. [S1–S6; through 2026-07-16]

## R9 — Competition, supply chain and regulation

- **R9.1 [FACT]** The Form 20-F describes competition from foundry service providers and IDMs on technology, capacity, quality, supply-chain diversification/resilience, customer satisfaction and pricing; it does not name Samsung or Intel. In the Q2 call, an analyst explicitly named Samsung Foundry and Intel and management answered at the category level, emphasizing technology, manufacturing and customer trust. Competitor node yields, capacity, pricing and share were **not obtained** from the primary TSMC set. [S1, Item 3; S6; through 2026-07-16]
- **R9.1A [FACT]** Management described frontend wafer fabrication and backend packaging as distinct competitive arenas and said alternative backend capacity could relieve TSMC’s packaging bottleneck and support its frontend wafer volume. [S6; 2026-07-16]
- **R9.2 [FACT]** TSMC identified silicon wafers, chemicals, gases and precious metals as major materials; some materials are sole-sourced. Tool access, raw-material availability/price, electricity, water, labor and skilled engineering are operating dependencies. Supplier-level spend and equipment/material unit costs were **not obtained**; annual-report supplier identities are anonymized. [S1, Items 3–4; S2, §5.3; FY2025]
- **R9.3 [FACT]** U.S. export controls may require licenses for certain advanced chips and destinations; the filing said a January 2025 rule could require licenses for products using 16nm or below unless conditions are met. TSMC Nanjing’s former VEU authorization expired in December 2025 and was replaced by an annual export license, whose renewal is not assured. [S1, Item 3; filing date 2026-04-16]
- **R9.4 [FACT]** TSMC said expanded export controls had not materially affected then-current results as of the annual-report date, while warning future trade tension, military conflict, sanctions, tariffs and controls could affect customers, supply chains, equipment/material access and demand. [S1, Item 3; 2026-04-16]
- **R9.5 [FACT]** The majority of TSMC’s principal production facilities and executives are in Taiwan; the filing identifies R.O.C./P.R.C. relations, war/hostilities, transport disruption, earthquakes, drought, electricity/water interruption and global-expansion execution as risks. Earthquakes caused approximately NT$3bn of losses in Q2 2024 and NT$5.3bn in Q1 2025, net of insurance. [S1, Item 3 and Note 37; through 2025-12-31]
- **R9.6 [FACT]** Geographic diversification itself carries higher construction/operating costs, fragmented ecosystem and personnel-transfer challenges. TSMC received or contracted for up to US$6.6bn direct U.S. CHIPS Act funding plus up to US$5bn proposed loans for Arizona, and up to €5bn German state aid for ESMC; receipt and retention are conditional. [S1, Item 3; 2026-04-16]

## 1. Business and reported segments

1. **[FACT] Reporting architecture.** Alphabet reports Google Services, Google Cloud and Other Bets. Revenue hedges and specified Alphabet-level costs sit outside segment results. See R1.1.
2. **[FACT] Google Services.** Advertising across Search & other, YouTube and Network is supplemented by consumer subscriptions, Play fees, devices and other services. See R1.2–R1.4.
3. **[FACT] Google Cloud.** GCP consumption/subscriptions, Workspace, cybersecurity, data/analytics, enterprise AI and TPU-system sales comprise the segment. See R1.5.
4. **[FACT] Other Bets.** This is an aggregate of individually immaterial operating segments, with revenue primarily from autonomous transportation and internet services. See R1.6.
5. **[FACT] Alphabet-level activities.** Shared frontier-model R&D and selected corporate costs are not allocated to the reported segments. See R1.1 and R3.

## 2. Segment driver trees

### 2.1 Google Search & other

1. **[DEDUCTED] Revenue bridge:** addressable queries × commercial-query mix × ad load × paid engagement rate × revenue per paid engagement, plus owned-property advertising outside core search.
2. **[FACT] Observable monetization bridge:** paid-click growth and cost-per-click growth are the filing KPIs; their latest and annual changes live in R2.2 and R2.
3. **[DEDUCTED] Query drivers:** user reach, device and geography mix, product relevance, default distribution, AI feature adoption, query length/complexity, and substitution toward competing search, commerce, social and AI products.
4. **[DEDUCTED] Monetization drivers:** advertiser demand, auction density, conversion quality, format/ad-load changes, vertical mix, geography/FX, and the ability to monetize AI-generated experiences.
5. **[DEDUCTED] Cost bridge:** TAC to distribution partners + serving/inference + content/operations + allocated infrastructure and product engineering. Search-only cost and profit are not obtained (R7.4, R9.1).
6. **[FACT] Current directional evidence.** Filing and call commentary attributes growth to query usage, advertiser spending and ad-format/delivery improvements; management also discusses AI features as incremental to query use. Use R2.1–R2.2 and R6.4 without converting adoption metrics into unsourced revenue.

### 2.2 YouTube advertising and subscriptions

1. **[DEDUCTED] Advertising bridge:** viewers × watch time × monetizable share × ad impressions per hour × price per impression, separated by Shorts, in-stream, connected TV/living room and other formats.
2. **[DEDUCTED] Subscription bridge:** paid subscribers × realized ARPU, separated where possible among Music/Premium, TV, Sunday Ticket and other offerings.
3. **[FACT] Revenue disclosure.** YouTube ads is reported separately; subscription revenue remains embedded in subscriptions, platforms, and devices. See R2 and R9.2.
4. **[DEDUCTED] Cost bridge:** creator/content revenue share and licensed content + serving/delivery + sales/support + allocated infrastructure, less any cross-product benefit to the broader Google ecosystem.
5. **[FACT] Current directional evidence.** Direct response and brand are named advertising drivers, while YouTube subscription growth is cited within the combined non-advertising line. Model-ready engagement, subscriber and cost series are not obtained (R2.1, R2.3, R9.2).

### 2.3 Google Network

1. **[DEDUCTED] Revenue bridge:** partner ad inventory × monetized impressions × price per impression, with separate logic for AdSense, AdMob and Ad Manager where data becomes available.
2. **[FACT] Filing KPIs:** Network impressions and cost-per-impression changes are disclosed; revenue is separately reported. See R2 and R2.2.
3. **[DEDUCTED] Profit bridge:** gross advertiser billings less TAC/revenue share to Network partners, serving expense, sales/support and allocated infrastructure.
4. **[FACT] TAC intensity is structurally higher on Network than Search & other, but Network-only TAC and profit are not disclosed. See R7.3–R7.4.
5. **[FACT] Current direction.** The filing identifies AdSense weakness and partial AdMob offset; the constituent dollars are not obtained (R2.1, R9.3).

### 2.4 Google subscriptions, platforms, and devices

1. **[DEDUCTED] Subscription bridge:** paid subscribers × realized ARPU × recognized months, separated by YouTube and Google One products.
2. **[DEDUCTED] Platform bridge:** Play transaction value × net service-fee rate, plus other platform fees.
3. **[DEDUCTED] Device bridge:** units × realized ASP, less returns and incentives; margin follows bill-of-materials, manufacturing/logistics, warranty and launch mix.
4. **[FACT] The combined revenue line is obtained, but its constituent revenue, units and profit are not. See R1.4, R2, R2.3 and R9.4.
5. **[DEDUCTED] Watchpoint:** growth in the combined line cannot by itself identify whether higher-margin subscriptions or lower-margin hardware generated the change.

### 2.5 Google Cloud

1. **[DEDUCTED] Services revenue bridge:** committed and on-demand consumption × realized unit price, plus Workspace and other seat subscriptions × realized price.
2. **[DEDUCTED] TPU-system revenue bridge:** contracted systems × delivered/accepted systems × recognized hardware/software value, with installation, support and warranty recognized according to service delivery.
3. **[FACT] Demand indicators:** reported Cloud revenue, operating income, backlog and recognition window live in R2–R3 and R6.
4. **[DEDUCTED] Backlog bridge:** opening backlog + new commitments and contract changes − recognized revenue − cancellations/expirations. The reporting-definition change prevents a clean sequential series without adjustment (R6.2, R9.8).
5. **[DEDUCTED] Profit bridge:** services revenue less compute, energy, network, support and allocated infrastructure, plus TPU-system gross profit, then employee and go-to-market expense. Third-party capacity, acquired-business costs, depreciation and product mix can alter margin.
6. **[FACT] GCP, Workspace, Wiz and TPU product dollars and margins are not separately obtained. See R9.5.

### 2.6 Other Bets

1. **[DEDUCTED] Waymo bridge:** paid rides × realized revenue per ride, plus partnership/other revenue; costs include fleet, mapping, remote assistance, insurance, maintenance, depreciation and market launch.
2. **[DEDUCTED] Other company bridges:** Wing deliveries × revenue per delivery; internet-service subscribers × ARPU; health/drug-discovery economics according to milestone, licensing or product contracts.
3. **[FACT] Alphabet reports only aggregate Other Bets revenue and operating loss. Company-level revenue, cost and operating KPIs are not obtained (R1.6, R3, R9.6).
4. **[DEDUCTED] External funding and valuation events should not be treated as operating revenue or proof of unit economics.

### 2.7 Corporate and consolidated items

1. **[DEDUCTED] R&D:** technical headcount/compensation and SBC + model development/training and serving infrastructure + product engineering + acquired R&D.
2. **[DEDUCTED] Sales and marketing:** sales headcount and commissions + partner/customer programs + consumer and advertiser promotion.
3. **[DEDUCTED] G&A:** corporate compensation and SBC + legal/regulatory charges + professional services + other administration.
4. **[FACT] Company-level functional expense is disclosed, while segment functional opex and capex are not. See R3.1, R4 and R9.7.
5. **[DEDUCTED] Non-operating gains should be separated from recurring operating earnings because investment remeasurement can dominate reported net income. See R4.1.

## 11. Competitive and regulatory frame

1. **[FACT] Search/ads.** Competition spans general and vertical search, commerce, social, AI services and online/offline advertising. See R8.1.
2. **[FACT] YouTube/subscriptions/devices.** Digital video, social, content/app platforms and hardware ecosystems are named competitor sets. See R8.1.
3. **[FACT] Cloud/AI.** Enterprise-cloud and AI model/product providers compete for customers, talent and infrastructure. See R8.1 and the capacity risks underlying R5.8.
4. **[FACT] U.S. Search case.** Liability, final judgment, remedies and appeals are summarized in R8.2.
5. **[FACT] Ad tech.** U.S., EC and UK matters can affect publisher tools, conduct and potentially structure; see R8.3–R8.4.
6. **[FACT] Android/Play/DMA.** Distribution, billing, interoperability and data-use constraints are in R8.5–R8.7.
7. **[FACT] Privacy and AI regulation.** Consent, opt-out, data portability, interoperability and product-change risks are in R8.9.
8. **[DEDUCTED]** Regulatory remedies should be mapped to the affected driver—distribution/TAC, query access, ad-tech take rate, Play fees, data advantage or product design—rather than applied as an undifferentiated discount.

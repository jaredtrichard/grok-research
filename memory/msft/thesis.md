# Microsoft working thesis

GF-MSFT-1 · as of 2026-09-29 · name id `msft`

Numbers live in the workbook. This file owns the claim, mechanism, and killing conditions. Official 12-month price target: [`models/msft/valuation.md`](../../models/msft/valuation.md). Do not treat any other figure as a second target.

## Claim

Microsoft’s operating momentum is real, but the last close requires more than the already-demanding base model. The model embeds Azure/AI-led IC growth of 30% / 24% / 20% through FY2029, PBP growth of 13% / 14% / 13%, FY2027 company revenue and operating-income growth above management’s double-digit bar, less than one point of FY2027 operating-margin compression, and positive free cash flow despite elevated capex. Even then, the DCF does not reach the tape: holding explicit cash flows and WACC fixed, the last close requires a 5.25% terminal growth rate versus the model’s 4.0%.

The thesis is therefore about duration and return on AI capital, not a denial of AI demand. Microsoft must convert constrained Azure demand, Copilot seats, and commercial RPO into sustained cash returns after capacity catches up. The filing establishes demand and growth; it does not yet establish standalone AI/Copilot gross profit, utilization, or ROIC (R9).

If the model does not say it, it is not the thesis. There is no separate unreported AI valuation wedge.

## Why this view is right

1. **The base already gives substantial credit to the disclosed momentum.** FY2027E company revenue grows 16.6% and operating income 15.0%; IC grows 30%. That is consistent with management’s double-digit full-year bar, Q1 IC guide, and Azure acceleration—not a low-growth straw man. See `segments.md` and `income.md`.
2. **Capacity constraint validates demand but not steady-state returns.** Management said Azure demand exceeded supply and new efficiency/capacity was monetized quickly (R4.3/R7.3). That supports near-term conversion. It does not reveal revenue per capital dollar once supply expands or protect against later price/utilization pressure.
3. **The gross-margin burden is already visible.** FY2026 Microsoft Cloud gross margin fell to 66%, IC gross margin fell through the historical segment series, and Q4 pressure came from Azure mix and AI infrastructure ahead of demand (R2.3/R4.1). The model lets IC operating margin hold near 41% before improving; failure to recover gross margin would reduce cash returns.
4. **Copilot is adoption evidence, not a standalone P&L.** More than 30 million paid Microsoft 365 Copilot seats and faster additions matter, but standalone revenue, ARPU, usage revenue, churn, and gross profit are not obtained (R4.4/R9.2). The model captures monetization through PBP segment growth and margin, avoiding a second Copilot revenue layer.
5. **RPO is large but not equivalent to near-term high-return revenue.** Commercial RPO reached $678 billion and grew 25% excluding OpenAI, but duration, exact concentration, and conversion economics are not obtained (R4.2/R9.7). The model does not insert backlog as revenue.
6. **Useful-life accounting does not remove cash capital intensity.** The FY2027 useful-life change affects depreciation timing and lease classification; management still expected capex to grow. The model carries cash PP&E additions above the FY2026 filing level and values resulting FCFF, not an accounting-margin shortcut.

## What others miss or get wrong

1. **“Demand exceeds supply” is not the end of the valuation.** It is the beginning of a capacity/utilization/price/return bridge.
2. **Adding an AI SOTP wedge double counts the thesis.** Azure, GitHub, Microsoft 365 Copilot, and other AI products already flow through IC/PBP revenue and margins. A separate value is justified only if sourced economics are carved out of—not stacked on—the filed segments.
3. **Seat count is not revenue.** Public list price × paid seats ignores timing, discounts, bundles, premium-SKU mix, usage, churn, and inference cost.
4. **RPO is not revenue and OpenAI concentration matters.** Ex-OpenAI growth is the cleaner directional fact; undisclosed duration prevents a precise conversion schedule.
5. **Current earnings include investment volatility.** FY2026 GAAP net income benefited from OpenAI investments (R5.1). The forecast excludes future marks and the DCF is built from operating income.
6. **The terminal assumption is the fulcrum.** The terminal value is 91% of enterprise value in `valuation.md`; modest changes in durable growth or WACC dominate the target.

## Mechanism and magnitude

- **Mechanism:** PBP premium seats/usage and IC capacity utilization drive revenue; product/infrastructure efficiency and opex leverage defend segment operating margins; capex and operating capital convert those earnings into FCFF.
- **Operating path:** see `segments.md` for the three reported segments, `income.md` for company earnings, and `cashflow.md` for capex/free cash flow. IC is the principal growth engine; MPC contracts in FY2027 before a modest recovery.
- **Valuation:** see the sole official PT, FCFF bridge, market-implied terminal-growth check, and sensitivities in [`valuation.md`](../../models/msft/valuation.md). The signed gap to the last close is an output, not an instruction to fit assumptions.
- **Magnitude test:** the explicit model already reaches FY2029 operating income of $247.7 billion and equity free cash flow of $153.3 billion. Reaching the tape still requires greater duration/lower risk than the official 8.5% WACC and 4.0% terminal-growth case.

## Killing conditions

Statuses reflect evidence through 2026-09-29. Recheck on primary filings, releases, metrics, and official transcripts.

| kill | status | what would do it / what remains | when to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| IC growth materially exceeds the model without margin erosion | Open | Sustained growth above the 30% / 24% / 20% path while IC operating margin holds above the modeled 41% / 41% / 42% | each quarterly segment print; FY2027 10-K |
| AI/cloud unit economics become observable and superior | Open | Sourced Azure/AI revenue, utilization, gross profit, or ROIC that supports higher FCFF without double counting | 10-Q/10-K; IR metrics |
| Copilot monetization outruns inference cost | Open | Standalone paid-seat/usage revenue and gross profit materially above the PBP path | quarterly transcript/metrics; contract disclosures |
| Capex converts faster than modeled | Open | Cash capex below the path while IC capacity and revenue remain above it, or equity FCF materially exceeds `cashflow.md` | each cash-flow statement |
| Durable growth supports the tape’s reverse DCF | Open | Evidence supports the market-implied terminal-growth requirement at the same risk/capital intensity | annual model refresh |
| Base operating path breaks lower | Open | FY2027 company growth falls below double digits, margin declines by at least one point, or FCF turns negative | quarterly guidance and FY2027 results |
| RPO quality weakens | Open | Ex-OpenAI growth decelerates sharply, conversion stretches, cancellations rise, or disclosed concentration increases | quarterly RPO disclosure |
| Capacity/partner/regulatory risk rises | Open | Material datacenter delay, power/chip constraint, partner economics deterioration, antitrust remedy, or cybersecurity event changes returns | 8-K/10-Q/10-K and official updates |

A share-price move alone does not kill the thesis. A narrative without unit economics does not kill it. Do not retune the official target to the tape.

## 2. Company-issued near-term expectations bar

Source: Microsoft’s [FY2026 Q4 official call transcript](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4), dated 2026-07-29. Full details and amounts are in register R7.

1. **[FACT]** FY2027 management expected double-digit revenue and operating-income growth, operating margin down less than one point, mid- to high-single-digit opex growth, year-over-year capex growth, approximately 20% tax, and positive free cash flow.
2. **[FACT]** Q1 FY2027 guidance called for 16%–17% company revenue growth, 33%–34% IC growth, approximately 45% Azure constant-currency growth, 11%–12% PBP growth, and $12.2–$12.7 billion of MPC revenue.
3. **[FACT]** The same outlook embedded a stable sequential Microsoft Cloud gross margin, capex above $50 billion, high-teens full-year pressure in Windows OEM and Devices, and declines in transactional M365/Server products.
4. **[DEDUCTED]** The published bar is not a low-growth reset. It already requires Azure acceleration, double-digit company growth, and enough operating leverage to absorb the AI infrastructure build with less than one point of margin compression.

## 3. Street debate visible in the official transcript

The items below are not sell-side estimate figures; they are the questions named analysts chose to press on the company’s official Q4 call.

1. **[FACT] Model/platform value.** UBS asked how Microsoft benefits from open/custom model adoption while carrying large frontier-lab exposure. Management argued that Azure’s architecture can serve multiple model families. [Official transcript](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4)
2. **[FACT] Azure acceleration versus capacity.** Jefferies asked whether 43% Azure growth and the guided acceleration reflected underlying demand, constraints, or execution. Management said constraints remained and fleet/process efficiency was monetized quickly. [Official transcript](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4)
3. **[FACT] Overbuild and hardware inflation.** Bernstein asked how Microsoft protects returns if datacenter/chip capacity is overbuilt and component prices rise. Management emphasized short-lived asset flexibility, diverse demand, pricing, and efficiency. [Official transcript](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4)
4. **[FACT] Copilot breadth and monetization.** Morgan Stanley asked whether paid seats were moving beyond pilots and whether monetization would come from more seats, premium SKUs, or usage. Management described seat-plus-usage economics and E7/Agent 365. [Official transcript](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4)
5. **[FACT] AI capex return.** Goldman Sachs asked how capex ROI compares with the prior year and which silicon/monetization levers remain. Management pointed to TAM confidence, application/infrastructure efficiency, model diversification, and first-party silicon, but did not provide a numeric ROIC. [Official transcript](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4)

**[DEDUCTED]** The visible debate is therefore not “is demand growing?” It is whether constrained-demand growth converts into durable returns after supply expands, and whether Copilot monetization outruns the cost of inference and infrastructure.

## 2. Company-set near-term bar

| Q3 2026 guide **[FACT]** | company range |
|---|---:|
| Net sales | $197.0bn–$202.0bn |
| Year-over-year growth | 9%–12% |
| FX impact | approximately 80 bps unfavorable |
| Operating income | $22.5bn–$26.5bn |

- **[FACT]** Amazon said Prime Day timing depressed the reported Q3 growth comparison: excluding Prime Day from both periods, year-over-year growth would be nearly 400 basis points higher.
- **[FACT]** The operating-income guide assumes no energy-derivative remeasurement and no additional acquisitions, restructurings or legal settlements.
- **[FACT]** The latest numerical 2026 capex outlook found remained “about $200 billion,” published with FY2025 results on 2026-02-05; the Q2 release did not provide a revised annual number. See R6.5.
- **[VIEW]** The near-term hurdle is therefore two-dimensional: sustain strong operating growth while funding an exceptional infrastructure build. A revenue beat without evidence on capacity monetization and cash returns would not resolve the core debate.

## 3. Debate map

### AWS AI acceleration versus the investment burden

- **[FACT]** Primary evidence: latest AWS usage/growth, operating income, contractual commitments, PPE additions and D&A are in R2, R3.3–R3.4, R4.3 and R6.6.
- **[VIEW] Bull debate.** AI demand, large multi-year commitments and custom silicon can sustain faster growth, improve price/performance and deepen customer lock-in.
- **[VIEW] Bear debate.** Revenue recognition may lag commitments while power, chips, depreciation and financing arrive earlier; customer concentration and contractual pricing can constrain incremental returns.
- **[VIEW] What may be missed.** RPO is not an annual run-rate, but neither is current revenue a complete measure of contracted demand. The model needs a commissioning-and-utilization bridge, not a simple backlog multiple.

### Stores margin durability versus reinvestment

- **[FACT]** Primary evidence: segment profit history, paid units, seller mix, shipping cost and fulfillment/technology cost are in R2–R3.
- **[VIEW] Bull debate.** Regionalized inventory, route density, automation and mix toward seller services/ads/subscriptions can sustain higher Stores margins.
- **[VIEW] Bear debate.** Faster delivery, sharper prices, quick commerce, content and International expansion can recycle productivity gains into customer benefits rather than reported margin.
- **[VIEW] What may be missed.** Reported retail revenue understates marketplace merchandise volume because third-party sales are net-reported; conversely, seller-unit mix alone does not reveal take rate or contribution.

### Advertising as a high-margin mix engine

- **[FACT]** Primary evidence: Advertising is a sales group, not a reportable segment; its revenue history and latest growth are in R3, while standalone cost/profit are not obtained (R8.4).
- **[VIEW] Bull debate.** High-intent shopping traffic, closed-loop attribution and video inventory support durable growth and attractive incremental economics.
- **[VIEW] Bear debate.** Ad load, advertiser returns, privacy/competition rules, publisher consideration and content/infrastructure costs limit extrapolation.
- **[VIEW] What may be missed.** Assigning a pure-play ad margin to all disclosed Advertising revenue is not supported by the filing.

### Prime and marketplace flywheel versus hidden cost

- **[FACT]** Primary evidence: Subscription and third-party seller revenue, unit mix and unearned revenue are in R3; members, churn, GMV, take rate and Prime benefit cost are not obtained.
- **[VIEW] Bull debate.** Prime can raise frequency and retention while marketplace density expands selection and monetization across seller services and ads.
- **[VIEW] Bear debate.** Shipping/content benefits, seller fee pressure and marketplace remedies may absorb the fee and mix advantage.
- **[VIEW] What may be missed.** Prime should be valued on ecosystem contribution, but the disclosure does not permit a clean standalone P&L.

### Strategic AI investments and GAAP earnings quality

- **[FACT]** Anthropic/OpenAI cash investment and non-operating marks are in R7.1–R7.2.
- **[VIEW] Bull debate.** Strategic stakes can appreciate and reinforce AWS demand/model access.
- **[VIEW] Bear debate.** Large valuation gains are non-operating and non-cash; linked cloud commitments can create concentration or circularity concerns while additional funding consumes cash.
- **[VIEW] What may be missed.** The equity stakes and operating cloud relationships require separate valuation and cash-flow treatment. Neither reported net income nor AWS revenue alone captures both correctly.

## 4. What appears embedded versus what remains optional

- **[VIEW] Appears embedded qualitatively:** continued AWS and Advertising growth, profitable North America, positive International operating income and substantial 2026 infrastructure spending. These are already visible in reported results and company guidance, so a valuation cannot treat them as undiscovered.
- **[VIEW] Still debated:** the duration of AWS AI growth, terminal return on the 2026 capacity build, retail margin reinvestment, International steady-state margin and Advertising’s true incremental margin.
- **[VIEW] Optional but unquantified:** upside from custom chips, agentic services, Amazon Leo, Zoox, healthcare/devices and strategic-equity appreciation. No market-implied value for these was obtained.
- **[VIEW] Potentially underappreciated downside:** depreciation and power costs after capacity commissioning; debt/lease funding; AI-customer concentration; marketplace remedies; and the cash/non-cash divergence created by investment marks.

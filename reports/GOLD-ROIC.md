## Definition — miners vs royalties (denominator)

Needed so a later test can be run. Not a quality sermon.

**ROIC** here means NOPAT (or an after-tax operating profit the source names) divided by **invested capital**. The fight is the denominator. A high print that is only an accounting artifact does not make the stock go up.

**Producer (NEM, B, AEM, KGC, AGI, EQX, BTG):**
- Denominator is typically **equity + interest-bearing debt − excess cash**, plus whatever the source does with leases, NCI, and equity-method JVs (NGM, PV).
- The live dollars sit in **mine PP&E, construction-in-progress, and M&A goodwill**. Growth capex (Hope Bay, Great Bear, IGD, Goose, Phase X, Redbird) enters the denominator **before** ounces and FCF.
- Impairments **shrink** the denominator and can **lift** subsequent ROIC without the stock having made money. Paulson (2017) and Citi (2013) documented the industry writing off tens of billions — that is the trap.
- Reserves and resources are **not** marked on the balance sheet. A high-grade discovery can be economically huge and ROIC-invisible until capitalized.
- **NCI / JV math:** NEM prints NGM at **38.5%**; Barrick at **61.5%**. A consolidated ROIC that swallows 100% NGM assets against 61.5% or 38.5% earnings is not a peer-comparable number. Same problem on BTG Fekola Regional **65/35** vs Fekola Mine **80/20**.
- **AISC is not ROIC.** AISC is a unit-cost scorecard. It is not used here to rank the eight.

**Royalty / stream (FNV):**
- Denominator is mostly the **book cost of royalty and stream interests**, not the mine’s PP&E. Operating cost is small. ROIC will **look structurally higher** than a miner’s for the same ounce of gold in the ground.
- FNV sells **GEOs**. Mixing GEO ROIC with gold-koz miner ROIC is a broken test.
- A single stream can be a large slice of invested capital. FNV wrote Cobre Panamá to **nil** ($1,169.2m, YE 2023). After that write-off the remaining book ROIC **improves** while the stock had already taken the hit. Same impairment trap as miners, just fewer assets.

**Rule for any later test:** split the panel — producers vs FNV — or the royalty names win on construction. Use the **same** NOPAT and capital definition inside each bucket. Do not score a name on ROIC the year after a mega-impairment without showing the stock path **through** the write-down.

**TEST NOT RUN:** a desk-built ROIC series for the eight on 22 Aug 2026. That would need 10+ years of comparable NOPAT, invested capital (with a stated NCI/JV and impairment treatment), daily total returns, and a gold and peer benchmark. Not built.

## Test of “ROIC wins” as a stock-return predictor vs gold and vs peers

**Verdict: industry-level capital destruction vs gold is sourced. Cross-sectional “buy high-ROIC miners” as a stock-picking screen is TEST NOT RUN. Demote ROIC as a ranking rule. Keep the regime rule that actually paid: do not blow the capital account when the metal rolls over.**

### What was sourced (not a desk backtest)

**1. After 2011, the industry earned below its cost of capital and the stocks lost to gold.**
- Paulson & Co. / Marcelo Kim, Denver Gold Forum (Northern Miner, 24 Sep 2017): from 2010 to ~20 Sep 2017, gold **+20%**, average TSR of 13 large producers **underperformed gold by 65%**; GDX **−45%**, GDXJ **−58%**. Industry **returns on capital < cost of capital**. Cumulative impairments since 2010 **US$85bn**. Eight large M&A deals 2010–16 spent **US$36bn**; **80%** of transaction value impaired; those deals still cash-flow negative **$1.7bn**. Only **Randgold (then LON:RRS / NASDAQ:GOLD)** and **Polymetal (LON:POLY)** posted **positive** shareholder returns 2010–2016. The rest of the named list — including **Barrick, Newmont, Kinross, Agnico, Goldcorp, Iamgold, AngloGold, Gold Fields, Newcrest, Yamana, Eldorado** — posted **negative** TSR. That is a published TSR table vs gold, not a ROIC sort.
- Sprott (12 Jul 2023, *Gold vs. Gold Stocks*): GDX **underperformed bullion 2011–2023** (Bloomberg chart to 11 Jul 2023). CapEx surged 2012–13 after the 2011 gold peak; “doomed the industry to subpar returns on capital for the next five years.” They print a **BMO ROIC-of-gold-producers chart, 2005–2022**. The chart is industry-level. Sprott does **not** publish a high-ROIC vs low-ROIC long-short vs gold.
- MarketRealist (Jul 2018), citing CIBC: senior-gold **ROIC fell below industry peers 2012–2015**, then recovered toward historic norms. Again industry-level, not a name sort that beat gold.
- BCG (14 Aug 2015 / *Mining Journal* 29 May 2015): industry **destroying shareholder value since 2011**. TSR of **15** majors, Dec 2011–Dec 2014: top three **Polyus, Agnico-Eagle, Randgold**. Except Polyus, even the winners had **negative** TSR. BCG attributes the relative win to **strategy / focus**, not to a ROIC quintile. **Not a ROIC screen.**
- Citi Research, *Impaired* (2013): top-10 golds cumulatively impaired **~US$61bn** since 2000 (~51% of then market cap). Worst vs market cap: **KGC, ANG, ABX**. They plot historic **EVA (ROIC − WACC)** as negative for the sector and keep a bearish stance. This is **coincident value destruction**, not a tradable “buy high ROIC” signal.

**2. What the same papers do *not* show.**
- No paper fetched here sorts the current eight (or a survivor-bias-free gold universe) by trailing or forward ROIC and reports subsequent excess return vs gold and vs equal-weight peers.
- Paulson’s two positive-TSR names (Randgold, Polymetal) were the **capital-discipline** shops of that cycle. That is **consistent with** “don’t destroy capital,” not a published ROIC rank with IRRs.
- BCG’s 2011–14 TSR winners include **Agnico** — which Paulson still listed as **negative TSR 2010–16**. Window and peer set change the winner. That is why a desk must not invent a single ROIC-alpha number.

**3. What *does* show up in published stock-return work — and it is not ROIC.**
- Tufano (1998, *Journal of Finance*): average North American gold miner moves **~2% per 1% gold**. Exposure **falls** with hedging and diversification and **rises** with leverage. Gold, not ROIC, is the priced factor.
- Baur (2014, *Review of Financial Economics*): Australian gold-miner gold beta **~1** over 1980–2010 (lower than Tufano), time-varying, **cut by hedges**; asymmetric (larger on up-gold). Again gold beta, not ROIC.
- Bhanja/Dar/Paul (2019, *International Review of Economics & Finance*): gold-mining stocks behave **more like gold than like equities** (UK/US, wavelets).
- Baranowski & Pera (*Gospodarka Surowcami Mineralnymi*, 2011–2020 GDX-style sample): gold beta **1.98** vs equity beta **0.45**.

**Decision for this desk:** drop ROIC as a **must-have rank** of the eight. Keep **capital non-destruction** (no peak-cycle M&A, no dilution for ounces, no growth capex that the stock already punished) as a **must-avoid of the 2011–16 episode**. If someone later wants “ROIC wins,” they must run: annual ROIC for a fixed universe, 1y/3y/5y subsequent TSR minus gold and minus peer-median, with and without the year after a >10% capital write-down. **That test is not run here.**

## Rigid screen: must-have / must-avoid / still unproven — only what paid in the stock

No AISC ranking of the eight. No ROIC ranking of the eight.

### Must-have (the stock has paid for these)

1. **Gold beta that is not written off.** Unhedged (or lightly hedged) exposure so the name actually moves with the metal (Tufano 1998; Baur 2014; 21 Aug 2026 tape). A “quality” miner that sold the tape forward is a gold substitute with the upside cut.
2. **Capital account that survives a down gold tape.** The 2011–16 episode: ROIC < WACC + $85bn impairments + GDX −45% vs gold +20% (Paulson). The rule that paid is **do not emit shares or write cheques for ounces at the top**.
3. **Title / fiscal residual is priced.** Host can take control (B Loulo), kill a contract (FNV Cobre), or re-cut the split (BTG Regional 65/35). The **+22%** BTG permit print is the same factor with the sign flipped.
4. **Per-share claim on the ounces.** Sprott (2023): dilution is a first-order reason miners lagged gold. Buybacks that **shrink** the share count (NEM >100m / ~9% since Feb 2024; AEM using sale proceeds to offset Rupert paper) are the inverse. Do not credit “returns” that are just a higher gold price on a fatter share count.

### Must-avoid (the stock has paid the other way)

1. **Peak-cycle M&A for ounces.** Paulson eight-deal list; **KGC −21%** on Tasiast/Red Back; **EQX −7.3%/−7.7%** the day it issued the Orla paper. Scale is not a return.
2. **Unpriced host-control.** A Mali / Panama / (historical) resource-nationalism line that is a double-digit NAV or EBITDA slice (FNV ~20% EBITDA; Loulo ~15% of Barrick ounces when running).
3. **Hard gold forwards / prepays into a rising tape.** They kill the beta the desk is paying for (Tufano). AGI 50 koz @ $1,821 is the live leftover.
4. **Single-asset geotech with no spare mill feed — when the market still treats the name as “safe Canada.”** AGI YD **−17%** is the 2026 exhibit. AEM Barnat and NEM Cadia paid **~4–5%** because the rest of the book was still running.

### Still unproven (do not promote)

- **High ROIC as a sort of the eight vs gold or vs peers.** TEST NOT RUN. Demoted.
- **Low AISC rank.** Not used. GOLD-COMPARE AISC is not a return screen.
- **Royalty ROIC > miner ROIC ⇒ own FNV.** Denominator is different; Cobre still hit the stock.
- **Great Bear / Hope Bay / IGD / Goose ramp / Fourmile-into-NGM as return rules.** Clocks are scout facts. Stock episodes are missing or tiny (KGC −2.2% on Lobo-Marte).
- **YE 2026 Barrick NA IPO** as a priced catalyst. Colour, not a filing fact. TEST NOT RUN vs gold.

## Pitch

**This pitch is weak as a ROIC product.** High-vs-low ROIC as a stock-return sort vs gold or vs peers was **TEST NOT RUN**. ROIC is **demoted**. Do not buy the eight on ROIC or AISC.

**Process from here (what has paid in the stock):** gold-first unhedged beta, then a blow-up filter — peak-cycle ounce M&A, unpriced host-control (Mali/Panama), hard forwards/prepays, single-asset geotech the market still treats as safe. Then a capital account that survives a down tape and a per-share claim (dilution is why miners lagged gold).

**What we would not do:** rank ROIC or AISC; NAV at Kitco $4,602; treat YE 2026 IPO as a filing fact; mix FNV GEOs with miner koz; promote anyone off this note.

**Which of the eight that process would even look at:** none as a buy list. After the filter: **AEM** first (diversified book; Barnat was a ~4–5% print, not a thesis-break); **FNV** as a royalty sleeve only (not because royalty ROIC is proven). **NEM/B** only as the NGM pair. **AGI** only after Young-Davidson is no longer the story. **KGC** thin (Red Back still the caution). **EQX** and **BTG** not on this process (Orla paper / 65/35 + hedges). Firstmate can take this to the captain as a **process pitch, not a name pitch**.

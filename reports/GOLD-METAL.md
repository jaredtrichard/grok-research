## How gold moves

Gold is not an equity and it is not a miner. Price is the residual of **who is adding or subtracting ounces** against a slow mine-supply curve, marked in dollars, against a real-rate opportunity cost.

**Four WGC-style blocks (mechanism, then what 2024–H1’26 actually printed):**

1. **Opportunity cost (real yields, USD, Fed path).** Jefferies (Kitco, 4 Aug): “Higher real rates increase the opportunity cost of holding gold, and when the move is abrupt, gold sells off significantly.” The 2026 flip from 1–2 cuts to 1–2 hikes is their stated cause of the **~25% peak-to-trough**. WGC GDT Q2 and the 8 Jul ETF note say the same for **North American ETFs**: weaker price + higher inflation/rate expectations + a **stronger dollar**. TE 21 Aug is the inverse tape: **buybacks → lower yields / weaker dollar → gold through $4,600**. Direction of TIPS/DXY on Friday itself is **not independently marked**.
2. **Official sector.** Central banks do not run a quarterly P&L. WGC GDT Q2: **288.9t** official-sector demand in Q2 (**+62% y/y**, +411% q/q off a **revised** Q1 **56.5t**). Metals Focus **cut Q1 CB from 244t to 57t** and moved **187t into OTC** (erratum, Jul 2026) — do not use the old Q1 CB print. WGC still sees **another strong CB year, below 2025**. FY 2025 CB **863.3t** vs FY 2024 **1,092.4t**. Reported H1 buyers (WGC CB stats 4 Aug): Poland 82t, Uzbekistan 41t, China 40t, Kazakhstan 27t; Turkey −83t, Russia −44t on the *reported* series (GDT total includes an unreported estimate).
3. **Western vs Asian investment.** This is the swing, not jewellery. FY 2025 ETFs **+801.2t** (second-strongest year). Q2’26 ETFs **−44.8t**. H1 still **+$8bn** globally; Asia **+$12bn** (strongest H1 on record); North America **−$7.7bn** (weakest H1 since 2013). June alone **−$8.9bn / −74t**; holdings end-June **4,047t**; H1 AUM **$526bn (−6%)** on a lower price. Bar & coin Q2 **307.1t** (−3% y/y) after two very strong quarters (Q1 **476.8t**). OTC & other Q2 **327.1t** (+91% y/y) — WGC’s H2 growth comment is **OTC + Asia**, not a Western ETF re-acceleration.
4. **Jewellery / recycle / mine (slow).** Jewellery consumption Q2 **278.2t**, lowest quarterly volume since the pandemic (−17% y/y) while **value** was still up (WGC: jewellery spend +14% y/y to $40bn). Recycle Q2 **326.1t** (−6% y/y, −13% q/q) as the q/q price drop **discouraged** scrap. Mine production Q2 **965.6t** (+2% y/y). WGC: only **modest** growth potential from mine + recycle. That is why a $4,000–$5,600 tape does not flood ounces.

**What WGC said at mid-year (1 Jul, reference week ending 26 Jun, not a 21 Aug forecast):** with moderate growth / cooling-but-elevated inflation / limited further tightening, gold “likely stay relatively rangebound (±5%)”; upside toward **$4,500+** if growth worsens, geo shocks, lower-rate expectations, or dip-buying; more than **10%** further downside “may be tempered by bargain-hunting.” That was written when LBMA was near the **$4,000** June low. The 21 Aug $4,600 tape is already back into that “$4,500 or above” band. It is **not** a new January regime.

**Geopolitics** (Kitco/Jefferies, WGC, TE): US–Iran / Hormuz / oil is a **regime** input — inflation, hike odds, and safe-haven at once. Not a quantified beta. TE 21 Aug: oil still **limits** the cut-odds bid even as the fiscal/buyback tape helps gold.

## Metal → equity transmission

A metal call that does not change the **sleeve** is incomplete. Transmission is mechanical. It is **not** a desk backtest and **not** a name rating.

### 1. Beta is not 1. The thing a miner sells is the **margin**, not the ounce.

Identity (WGC AISC definition, 2013+): **margin/oz ≈ realized gold − AISC**. AISC already includes site costs, sustaining capex, **royalties**, and corporate allocations (company definitions still differ — GOLD-COMPARE).

If AISC were frozen, % change in margin ≈ (% change in gold) × (gold / margin). That is **torque**. It is larger when the mine is closer to the cost line and smaller when the spread is already wide.

**AISC is not frozen.** WGC (Tomlinson, Mar 2025, on Q3’24 industry costs): as the gold price rises, (i) **lower-grade** rock becomes economic, (ii) **royalties and mining tax** rise with revenue/profit, (iii) better margins fund **sustaining capex**. Q3’24 global royalties/mining-tax unit cost **$90/oz** (+31% y/y); sustaining-capex unit **$303/oz** (+50% y/y); industry AISC then **$1,456/oz**. The same sentence is in the company books: Barrick Q2’26 (B-SCOUT / GOLD-COMPARE) attrib. AISC **$1,866/oz** on realized **$4,417**; cost of sales up “higher royalties associated with the stronger realized gold price” plus grades and fuel (Barrick Q2 language as carried in the scout pack). AngloGold (not in the eight; cited only as a **mechanism** print) Q1’26 AISC **$1,955** vs $1,640, “mainly from gold price-linked royalties.”

So **equity gold-beta < textbook torque**. Street “3× miner” is a fixed-cost cartoon. We do **not** estimate a sleeve beta here.

### 3. Royalties / streams vs miners (why FNV is a different gold instrument)

- **Miner:** pays the cost line. Gets operating leverage **and** cost inflation (royalties, energy, grade). Capex and jurisdiction sit in the equity. Hedge/prepay books **cap** realized (AGI residual **50 koz @ $1,821** into 1H 2027; BTG collars through Jan 2027, Q2 realized loss **$71m**; EQX prepays/swaps still below tape into Sep 2026 — all GOLD-COMPARE).
- **NSR royalty (FNV book):** a **% of net smelter revenue**. No mine-site AISC. Gold-price beta on that slice is close to **1 on revenue**, with almost no opex torque. Operator cost inflation does **not** hit an NSR the way it hits a miner — except the **profit-based** slice (FNV-SCOUT / GOLD-COMPARE: profit-based **12% of H1 revenue**), which *does* see operator costs.
- **Stream:** FNV pays an ongoing delivery price (Q2 **$40.6m of $45.9m** cash cost). Spread widens when spot rises, but not dollar-for-dollar. FNV 2026 GEOs use a **fixed $4,500** conversion; 2025 GEOs are **not** like-for-like.
- **Who pays whom:** the same gold-price-linked royalties that **inflate miner AISC** are, in part, **revenue** to the royalty sleeve. That is the transmission. It is why a metal-up tape that also inflates royalties is **friendlier to FNV than to a high-royalty-jurisdiction miner**, all else equal.

Cobre Panamá is **stockpile sales guided to start Q3**, not a restart (FNV 11 Aug). That is a **volume** item, not a metal-beta item.

**Sourced lags:**

- **Realized vs spot.** Q2 company realized sat on the **Q2 LBMA avg (~$4,506)**, $150–$350 **below** the 21 Aug Kitco/TE tape (except BTG’s prepay $3,767). Q3 P&L, if this tape holds, prints **higher realized** than Q2. That is a **one-quarter lag** in the income statement, not a mystery.
- **Cost decks.** NEM/B/AEM/KGC/FNV GEO ruler **$4,500**; BTG **$5,000**; AGI Mexico cash-tax **$4,000**. Royalty and AISC **lines move with the deck**. A common-price restatement **does not exist** in the scout pack.
- **Reserve prices** are $1,350–$2,500. **Do not NAV the book at Kitco $4,602** (GOLD-COMPARE). NAV lag vs spot is structural; we have not built NAV.

**Sourced co-movement, not a lead:**

- Jefferies (4 Aug): “both gold **and gold equities** have already undergone a meaningful reset”; “when real-rate pressure subsides, gold **and gold equities** move higher.” They treat the sleeve as **the same trade as the metal** on the rate factor.
- WGC: Western ETF **tonnes** (GLD/IAU — instruments, not names) are the high-frequency link from real yields/USD into **the gold complex**. Equity desks often trade that flow, but we have **no** sourced GDX vs XAU lead/lag series in this pass.

**Not sourced — do not invent:**

- A GDX/XAU beta, a “miners lead gold by N days,” or any 2024–26 backtest.
- A claim that Barrick delivered “1.7×” as a law of the sleeve (secondary blogs derive it; we will not promote it).
- July/August ETF tonnes after WGC’s 8 Jul / mid-Aug tables (WGC data page notes a July 2026 xlsx; **not extracted here**).

### 5. What the 21 Aug metal tape therefore does to the eight **as a group**

- Metal is **above** the $4,500 guide deck most names use and **above** Q2 realized. If it **holds**, Q3 realized and royalty lines **catch up**. That is a **group** earnings tail, not a stock-pick.
- Metal is **not** back at the January ATH. Treating the eight like a fresh 2025 torque cycle (when gold went $2,400 → $4,100 → $5,400 and AISC had not fully followed) **overstates** senior torque and **understates** mid-tier leftover torque.

- Hedges and prepays (AGI, BTG, EQX) mean **group realized < spot** until those books roll off. Torque is **not** uniform across the eight.

## Working stance + what would change your mind

**This is still not an official view.**

**Metal from here:** **Constructive hold of the $4,500–$4,600 zone, not a new ATH call.** The 2024–25 trend ($2,386 → $3,432 annual avg, then Jan $5,400–$5,600) was an investment-and-CB regime. Q2 showed that regime **bends** when US real yields and the dollar reprice hiked. Friday is a **fiscal/USD bounce** back through the WGC mid-year “$4,500 or above” line, still **~15–18% below** the January highs depending on series. Base case while TIPS stay near the Jefferies 2.4% neighbourhood and official 21 Aug LBMA/COMEX are missing: **range $4,200–$5,000**, with $4,500 as the **deck/average** that already sits in the eight’s cost guides. Not a forecast model — a working band around sourced prints.

**What would make us more aggressive on metal (and therefore on group torque):** official LBMA/COMEX **holding above $4,800** with 10y TIPS **back toward ~1.94** (or clearly falling), NA ETF tonnes turning **in** (need a post-June WGC table), and hike odds fading **without** a Hormuz oil spike. That is a **re-opening of the 2025 tape**, and the eight should be held as a **risk-on gold sleeve**, not as a royalty bunker.

**What would make us less aggressive (and therefore cut group torque):** official print **back through $4,200** toward the June LBMA **~$4,002** low **with** TIPS still ≥2.4% and a **stronger dollar**; June-style NA ETF liquidation repeating; or Hormuz **resolution** that kills the haven bid **while** hike pricing stays. Then the eight are a **high-beta down-tape**, and we do not own the high-AISC third as if it were cheap torque.

**What does not change our mind:** a single CFD print, a secondary COMEX recap, or jewellery tonnes. CB staying “strong but below 2025” is already in the base.
